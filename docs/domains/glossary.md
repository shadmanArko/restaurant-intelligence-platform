# Domain Glossary

Version: 1.0
Status: Active

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the official business language of the Restaurant Operating System (ROS).

All documentation, code, APIs, events, reports, and AI systems must use these definitions consistently.

---

# Batch

A production run that converts raw ingredients into finished products.

Example:

Kacchi Batch #2025-001

Consumes:

- Rice
- Meat
- Oil
- Spices

Produces:

100 Portions

---

# Portion

A sellable quantity of a finished product.

Examples:

- 1 Portion Kacchi
- 1 Glass Borhani

Portions are sold to customers.

---

# Yield

The actual quantity produced from a batch.

Example:

Expected Yield:
100 Portions

Actual Yield:
95 Portions

Yield impacts profitability.

---

# Recipe

A formal definition of how a product is produced.

Defines:

- Ingredients
- Quantities
- Expected Yield

Recipes are versioned.

---

# Recipe Version

A snapshot of a recipe at a specific point in time.

Historical orders and batches must reference the recipe version used.

Recipes are never overwritten.

---

# Ingredient

A raw material used in production.

Examples:

- Rice
- Meat
- Oil
- Yogurt
- Spices

Ingredients are inventory items.

---

# Raw Inventory

Inventory that has not yet entered production.

Examples:

- Rice
- Meat
- Oil

Raw inventory is consumed by production batches.

---

# Finished Inventory

Inventory that is ready for sale.

Examples:

- Kacchi Portion
- Borhani Bottle

Finished inventory is consumed by orders.

---

# Inventory Movement

Any event that changes inventory.

Examples:

Purchase
Consumption
Adjustment
Transfer
Waste
Return

Inventory history is authoritative.

---

# Inventory Adjustment

A manual correction to inventory balances.

Adjustments must always be auditable.

---

# Waste

Inventory that cannot generate revenue.

Examples:

Spoilage
Damage
Expired Food
Operational Loss

Waste must be tracked separately.

---

# Variance

Difference between expected and actual consumption.

Formula:

Variance =
Actual Consumption
-
Expected Consumption

Variance helps identify:

- Waste
- Theft
- Over-serving
- Process Issues

---

# Production

The process of converting ingredients into finished products.

Example:

Rice + Meat + Oil
→
Kacchi

---

# Production Batch

A single production execution.

Contains:

- Recipe Version
- Expected Yield
- Actual Yield
- Actual Consumption

---

# Menu Item

A product available for sale.

Examples:

- Kacchi
- Borhani
- Combo Meal

A menu item may reference one or more finished products.

---

# Customer

A person or organization that places orders.

Customer history is permanent.

---

# Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

Total revenue generated by a customer over time.

---

# Order

A customer request to purchase products.

Orders are immutable business records.

---

# Order Item

A single line within an order.

Example:

2 × Kacchi

1 × Borhani

---

# Sales Channel

Origin of an order.

Examples:

- Website
- Facebook
- WhatsApp
- Wolt
- Uber Eats
- Walk-In
- Phone

---

# Supplier

A vendor that provides ingredients or services.

Examples:

- Rice Supplier
- Meat Supplier

---

# Purchase

Acquisition of inventory from a supplier.

---

# Cost Of Goods Sold (COGS)

The inventory cost consumed by sales.

COGS contributes directly to profitability calculations.

---

# Revenue

Income generated from completed sales.

---

# Expense

A cost that is not inventory.

Examples:

- Rent
- Salary
- Electricity
- Marketing

---

# Journal Entry

A double-entry accounting record.

Journal entries are immutable.

---

# Ledger Account

A financial account used for accounting classification.

Examples:

- Cash
- Inventory
- Revenue
- Expense

---

# Branch

A physical operating location.

Examples:

- Cloud Kitchen
- Restaurant
- Central Production Kitchen

---

# Audit Log

An immutable record of important business actions.

Audit logs must never be modified.

---

# Event

A business fact that already happened.

Examples:

OrderPlaced

BatchCompleted

InventoryConsumed

Events are immutable.

---

# Intelligence

Insights generated from operational data.

Examples:

- Forecasting
- Waste Detection
- Customer Segmentation
- Profitability Analysis

---

# Source Of Truth

The authoritative owner of data.

Examples:

Inventory Domain
→ Inventory Data

Accounting Domain
→ Financial Data

There must never be multiple sources of truth for the same concept.
